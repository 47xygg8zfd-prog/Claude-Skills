# PM Presentations Skill

Create effective PM slide decks for every stakeholder communication scenario.

---

## Core Principle: Lead with the Answer

PMs often bury the point. Every deck should follow the **Pyramid Principle**:
1. **Lead with the recommendation / conclusion**
2. Support with data and reasoning
3. Anticipate objections

Executives read the first slide and decide if they need the rest. Make slide 1 count.

## Deck Templates

### 1. Executive Product Update

**Purpose**: Weekly/bi-weekly update to leadership on product progress
**Length**: 5–8 slides
**Tone**: Crisp, confident, data-driven

```
Slide 1: Status Summary
  - Overall status: 🟢 On Track / 🟡 At Risk / 🔴 Off Track
  - Top 3 things that happened this period
  - Top 3 things happening next period
  - 1 ask / decision needed from leadership

Slide 2: Key Metrics Snapshot
  - 3–5 KPIs with trend arrows (↑ ↓ →)
  - Period-over-period comparison
  - One sentence of context per metric

Slide 3: Milestone Progress
  - Timeline visual or table
  - Planned vs. actual dates
  - RAG status per milestone

Slide 4: Risks & Mitigations
  - Top 2–3 risks (table: Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Owner)
  - Escalations needed (if any)

Slide 5: Decisions Needed
  - Each decision on its own row: Decision | Options | Recommendation | Deadline
  - Be explicit — don't hide asks in bullet points

Slide 6: Appendix (optional)
  - Supporting data, deep-dives, backlog snapshots
```

### 2. Product Roadmap Deck

**Purpose**: Communicate the product direction to stakeholders, partners, or all-hands
**Length**: 8–12 slides
**Tone**: Visionary but grounded

```
Slide 1: Title
  - Product / Team name
  - Time horizon (e.g., "H2 2025 Roadmap")
  - Presenter + date

Slide 2: Our North Star
  - Mission / vision statement
  - The customer problem we're solving

Slide 3: Strategy Pillars
  - 3 strategic bets / themes for the period
  - One visual per pillar (icon + 2-sentence description)

Slide 4: Last Quarter: What We Shipped
  - Highlights only — 4–6 bullets with outcomes, not features
  - Format: "Launched X → resulted in Y"

Slide 5–7: Roadmap by Quarter (or Now/Next/Later)
  - Per time period: committed items + outcomes
  - Confidence level (Committed / Likely / Exploratory)
  - Don't list every story — just the themes and key deliverables

Slide 8: Success Metrics
  - How we'll know the roadmap delivered value
  - 3–5 KPIs with targets

Slide 9: Dependencies & Risks
  - What we need from other teams
  - Top risks and how we're managing them

Slide 10: What's NOT on the Roadmap (optional but powerful)
  - Explicitly calling out deprioritized work builds trust

Slide 11: Q&A / Open Questions
```

### 3. Sprint Review / Demo Deck

**Purpose**: Show stakeholders what shipped in the sprint
**Length**: 5–10 slides (one per story/feature)
**Tone**: Show, don't tell

```
Slide 1: Sprint Summary
  - Sprint #, dates
  - Sprint goal: Did we achieve it? ✅ / ⚠️ / ❌
  - Stories committed: X | Completed: Y | Velocity: Z pts

Slide 2: What We Built (Overview)
  - List of completed stories (title + 1-line outcome)
  - Separate section for "Carried Over"

Slides 3–N: Demo Slides (one per key feature)
  Per feature:
  - Problem: Why we built this
  - Screenshot / recording / GIF
  - Impact: How it moves the needle

Slide N+1: Metrics Moved
  - Any early signals on impact

Slide N+2: What's Coming Next Sprint
  - Top 3–5 priorities
  - Any new risks or blockers to flag

Slide N+3: Feedback / Q&A
```

### 4. Feature Pitch / Product Brief

**Purpose**: Get buy-in for a new feature or initiative
**Length**: 6–8 slides
**Tone**: Persuasive, data-backed

```
Slide 1: The Opportunity
  - One-sentence problem statement
  - Scale of the problem (user data, market data)
  - Why now?

Slide 2: User Insight
  - Who is affected
  - Quote / story from a real user
  - Quantify the pain: "X% of users churn at step Y"

Slide 3: Proposed Solution
  - What we're building (high-level, no wireframes yet)
  - What it enables the user to do
  - Screenshot or mockup if available

Slide 4: Why This Approach
  - Alternatives considered and why ruled out
  - Key tradeoffs

Slide 5: Success Metrics
  - Primary metric (the one that matters most)
  - Secondary / guardrail metrics
  - Measurement plan

Slide 6: Scope & Timeline
  - MVP vs. future phases
  - T-shirt sizing or confidence interval
  - Dependencies

Slide 7: Ask
  - What decision / resource / approval do you need?
  - Be explicit: "We need X by Y to hit Z"
```

### 5. Quarterly Business Review (QBR)

**Purpose**: Review performance against OKRs for the quarter
**Length**: 12–18 slides
**Tone**: Honest, analytical, forward-looking

```
Slide 1: Title
Slide 2: Executive Summary — the 60-second version of the whole deck
Slide 3: Last Quarter's Goals — what we set out to do
Slides 4–8: OKR Scorecard (one slide per KR)
  - Target vs. Actual
  - Why we hit/missed
  - Key learnings
Slide 9: What We Shipped — top 5 launches with outcomes
Slide 10: What We Learned — experiments, pivots, surprises
Slide 11: What Slowed Us Down — honest retrospective
Slide 12: Next Quarter's Goals
Slide 13: Resource Ask (headcount, budget, tooling)
Slide 14: Risks
Slide 15: Appendix
```

---

### 6. Retro Summary Deck

**Purpose**: Share retro output with the team or leadership
**Length**: 3–5 slides
**Tone**: Transparent, action-oriented

```
Slide 1: Sprint Recap
  - Sprint goal outcome
  - Velocity, completion rate

Slide 2: What Went Well
  - Top themes from Glad/Start/Continue
  - Specific wins to acknowledge

Slide 3: What Needs Improvement
  - Top themes from Mad/Sad/Stop
  - Root causes identified

Slide 4: Action Items
  - Table: Action | Owner | Due Date | Status
  - Review last sprint's action items (closed / carried / missed)

Slide 5 (optional): Team Health / Morale Signal
```

## Slide Writing Rules

1. **One idea per slide** — if you're tempted to add "and also", make a new slide
2. **Slide title = the point** — "Q3 Revenue Was $2.4M (↑18% QoQ)" not "Revenue"
3. **3×3 rule** — max 3 bullets per slide, max 3 words per bullet (in exec decks)
4. **Data beats assertions** — "Usage doubled" beats "Customers love it"
5. **Visualize trends, tabulate comparisons** — don't use a table for a trend; don't use a line chart for a 3-column comparison
6. **Appendix generously** — when in doubt, put it in the appendix so the deck stays clean

---

## Slide Tone by Audience

| Audience | Tone | Detail Level | Visuals |
|----------|------|-------------|---------|
| C-suite / Board | Strategic, outcome-focused | Low — conclusions only | KPIs, trend lines |
| VP / Director | Strategic + tactical | Medium | Charts + tables |
| Engineering | Technical, precise | High | Architecture diagrams, timelines |
| All-hands | Inspiring, accessible | Medium | Screenshots, stories |
| External (customers, partners) | Professional, benefit-focused | Low–medium | Product screenshots |

## Building the Deck

When the user asks for a slide deck, follow this workflow:

1. **Identify the template** — which of the above fits?
2. **Gather inputs** — ask for: audience, goal, key data points, deadline
3. **Draft the outline** — share slide-by-slide outline for approval before building
4. **Build using pptx skill** — see `/mnt/skills/public/pptx/SKILL.md` for implementation
5. **Review** — check that every slide title is a statement, not a topic

### Quick Deck Request Response Pattern
```
"I'll build a [template type] deck. Before I write the slides, I need:
1. Audience: Who's in the room?
2. Key message: If they remember one thing, what is it?
3. Data: [specific numbers or metrics needed]
4. Length: Any slide count constraints?
```
