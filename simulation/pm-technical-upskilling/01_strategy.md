# Strategic Framing: TechBridge — PM Technical Upskilling Platform

## Strategic Fit

- **OKR alignment**: Supports Objective 2 (Expand TAM beyond analytics tooling) and KR: Enter the PM productivity/enablement category with a new product line by Q3
- **Where to play**: Product managers at B2B SaaS companies (50–500 engineers) who work closely with engineering teams — specifically PMs who feel blocked or underconfident in technical conversations
- **How to win**: The only upskilling tool built around the PM-engineer relationship, not generic coding education. We win by making technical knowledge immediately applicable to the PM's actual job, not by teaching them to code.

## Investment Thesis

The PM upskilling market is underserved at the intersection of product and engineering. Existing solutions (Codecademy, Udemy, YouTube) teach general programming — not how to read a pull request, understand a system architecture diagram, or have a productive conversation about technical debt. A tool designed specifically for PMs, around PM workflows, has no direct competitor and addresses a pain point that 70%+ of PMs report as limiting their effectiveness. A freemium PLG model mirrors our existing motion and the TAM is large: 500K+ PMs globally at B2B SaaS companies.

## Prioritization Signal

**Tier**: P1 — this quarter, strategic bet
**Rationale**: Core analytics product is mature; this extends our PM user base into a new use case without requiring a full platform rebuild. Low technical risk, high strategic optionality.

## Constraints

- **Budget**: 2 engineers + 1 designer for initial build; can expand to 4 engineers at Series B milestone
- **Timeline**: No hard launch deadline, but must show early traction within one quarter of launch to justify continued investment
- **Risks**: (1) PMs may want this but not pay for it — validate willingness to pay early. (2) Content quality is hard to sustain; must be opinionated rather than comprehensive.

## Green Light Conditions

Discovery must confirm: (1) PMs feel blocked by technical gaps in their current job, not just abstractly interested in learning; (2) at least one specific, recurring workflow where technical knowledge would directly improve their output; (3) PMs are willing to spend time on this during the workday, not just as personal development.
