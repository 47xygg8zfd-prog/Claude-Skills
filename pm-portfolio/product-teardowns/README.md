# Product Teardowns

A product teardown is a structured competitive analysis — not a feature list, but a PM-level read of why a product exists, who it's built for, and where it's vulnerable. Good teardowns make you a better builder. They force you to ask uncomfortable questions: why did they make that tradeoff? what does their retention loop assume about user behavior? where is the wedge a competitor could drive?

Each teardown here follows the same framework: **problem → segment → onboarding → retention → monetization → weaknesses → opportunity**. The goal isn't comprehensiveness — it's sharpness. Where does the product win? Where does it leave users underserved? And, critically, what would I build next if I were their PM?

These teardowns were written from the perspective of a PM at Pulse, a B2B team analytics platform for engineering managers. That POV shapes the analysis — especially the competitive counter sections — but the goal is honest intelligence, not marketing. A teardown that only flatters your own product teaches you nothing.
