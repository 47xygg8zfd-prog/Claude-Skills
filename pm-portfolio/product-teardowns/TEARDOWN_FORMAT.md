# Product Teardown Format

## Structure

Each teardown follows this structure to reveal strategic intent, not just features:

### TL;DR
**1-2 sentence blunt, opinionated insight** — the most surprising or counterintuitive thing about this product. Should make the reader want to keep reading.

### What This Product Is Really Optimizing For
NOT what the product says it does — what it's actually designed to maximize, revealed by examining the design choices, default views, pricing triggers, and growth mechanics. One paragraph, opinionated.

### Jobs to Be Done
| Job type | The job | What users hired before | Why this product wins this job |
|----------|---------|------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Functional | | | |
| Emotional | | | |
| Social | | | |

### Target Segment
- **Primary**: Specific role, company stage, context (not just "developers")
- **Secondary**: Who else benefits
- **Explicitly not served**: Who they've deprioritized, and the strategic trade-off

### Onboarding & The Aha Moment
- **Day 1 flow**: Step by step — what happens, what friction exists
- **The aha moment**: Specific interaction where value becomes undeniable
- **Time to aha**: Fast / Slow — and whether that's a problem
- **What they're betting on**: The belief about users driving the flow design

### The Growth Loop
ASCII diagram showing the acquisition and retention mechanics, with annotations.

**Loop type**: Viral / Content / Paid / Product-led / Network effects / etc.
**Loop strength**: Strong / Moderate / Weak — why
**Leakage point**: Where most users fall out

### Retention Mechanics
- **What brings users back**: Specific trigger
- **Retention curve shape**: Describe and interpret
- **The habit they're building**: Specific behavior they want to become automatic
- **Churn signals**: What early churn looks like

### Monetization & Strategic Alignment
- **Model**: Per seat / Usage / Tiered / Freemium / etc.
- **Free tier purpose**: Why they give this away
- **Upgrade trigger**: Specific moment or limit
- **Alignment check**: Does monetization reinforce or undermine retention?

### Key Metrics & What They Reveal
*(Added after initial research)*

- **North Star metric**: The one number this product is built around
- **How you know**: Which product behaviors/design choices reveal this metric is primary
- **Input metrics**: What the team is measuring to move the north star
- **What this tells us**: What the metric choice reveals about their strategy and constraints

### Feature Strategy
For 3-5 non-obvious features: what it does, and the **strategic bet** behind it.

| Feature | What it does | The strategic bet |
|---------|-------------|------------------|
| [Feature] | [one sentence] | [Why this feature exists — what belief it represents] |

### Weaknesses & Vulnerabilities
**[Weakness 1]**: Specific, with stakes — what could go wrong
**[Weakness 2]**: Specific
**[Weakness 3]**: Specific

### 3 Lessons for Any PM
1. **[Lesson title]**: Specific, transferable insight — something a PM could apply tomorrow
2. **[Lesson title]**: Specific
3. **[Lesson title]**: Specific

### If I Were PM Here
One paragraph. Specific recommendation — what you'd build next, why, and what metric it moves. Connect it to a weakness or growth loop leakage point identified above.

---

## What Makes a Great Teardown

- **Opinionated**: Takes a position on what the product is really doing
- **Specific**: Names names, uses real metrics, references actual behaviors
- **Honest**: Includes where competitors win, not just weaknesses
- **Strategic**: Every section reveals intent, not just describing features
- **Actionable**: The 3 lessons are things you could apply tomorrow to your own product
- **Visual**: Diagrams (growth loops) make complex systems graspable
- **Connected**: Feature choices trace back to what the product is optimizing for
